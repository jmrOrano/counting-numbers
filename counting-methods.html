Order, then repetition.

Permutation and combination aren't separate topics to memorize — they're two answers on one decision tree. Ask does order matter? then can items repeat? and the formula falls out on its own.

03 — Reference

The four formulas

Every formula below is built from the same two variables.

n number of possible items available to choose from
r number of slots/choices you're actually arranging or picking
Order matters · no repetition
Permutation
ₙPᵣ= n!(n−r)!
Choosing a President, VP, and Secretary from 5 people. Position matters, and each person can only hold one role.
Order matters · repetition allowed
Ordered with repetition
nr
A 4-digit PIN from 10 possible digits. Every slot independently has n choices, so you just multiply n by itself r times.
Order doesn't matter · no repetition
Combination
ₙCᵣ= n!r!(n−r)!
Picking 3 committee members from 10 candidates. Same group regardless of the order you picked them in.
Order doesn't matter · repetition allowed
Combination with repetition
ⁿ⁺ʳ⁻¹Cᵣ
Choosing 4 scoops of ice cream from 10 flavors, repeats allowed, order you scoop them doesn't matter.
04 — Worked examples

From the notes

Permutation 5 people, 3 positions
There are 5 people, and you're picking and arranging just 3 positions. Person A as President is different from Person A as VP — order matters, no repeats since each person fills one role.
₅P₃= 5!(5−3)! = 5!2! = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60
Combination Choosing 2 from {A, B, C}
Order doesn't matter here — AB and BA are the same selection. Out of 6 possible orderings, they collapse into 3 unique pairs.
₃C₂= 3!2!(3−2)! = 3
n^r 4-digit brute force, 10 digits
The case that started this whole map. 4 slots, 10 possible digits per slot, order matters (1234 ≠ 4321), and repetition is allowed (1111 is valid). Not ₙPᵣ, not ₙCᵣ — every slot is an independent choice.
104 = 10,000 possible outcomes
05 — Before reaching for a formula

Ask these first

1
What am I counting?
Arrangement / sequence / positions → leans permutation. Selection / group / set → leans combination.
2
Does order matter?
Swap two items — is the result a different outcome? If yes, order matters.
3
Is repetition allowed?
Can the same item be used more than once in one outcome? PINs and passwords usually yes; picking people usually no.

Order → repetition → formula. Classify first, calculate last.
